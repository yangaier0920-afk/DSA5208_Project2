# DSA5208 Project 2: concise results summary

This summary is shorter than the full report. See Final_Report_EN.md / Final_Report.pdf for figures, tables, methods and limitations.

This project uses the eight annual Singapore rainfall datasets from 2017 to 2024 to address two tasks: describe within-year and interannual rainfall patterns, and estimate the probability that a station will accumulate more than an input threshold during the next 30 minutes. Apache Spark performs structured processing and feature computation; Spark MLlib trains and evaluates the probability models. All times use Asia/Singapore.

2018 has no station meeting 90% annual coverage and is kept as NA. The eight-year fixed annual station intersection is empty. Descriptive means range from approximately 1,667.9 mm in 2019 to 2,898.9 mm in 2022 among each year's qualifying stations, but these are observed totals from changing station sets. They are not comparable estimates of national annual totals, nor evidence of a long-term climate trend. Requiring every month to pass coverage eliminates the entire annual panel in several years, demonstrating why an annual average coverage rule can hide concentrated missingness.

At the 90% rule, November has the highest descriptive wet-slot fraction (4.30%), while March has the highest mean station-month nonzero five-minute P90 (2.84 mm). Thus rain occurrence and conditional intensity need not peak in the same month. Spark percentile_approx uses accuracy=1000; station-month nonzero quantiles are averaged across qualified stations and available years. They are not quantiles of all pooled national observations. All-dry groups have wet frequency zero but unknown conditional intensity, not a zero-filled intensity.

Only May, September, October and December have supported eight-year panels. The lowest/highest years agree at both coverage cutoffs for each supported month. For October's 21-station 90% panel, the 2022 mean is 399.8 mm and the 2023 mean is 113.0 mm. May peaks in 2024, September in 2020, October in 2022 and December in 2023; no single year is wettest everywhere. Adding these four months would not create an annual comparison. The 95% cutoff changes composition and means, so stable extrema are sensitivity evidence rather than a significance test.

All seven adjacent-year pairs and 12 months were fixed in advance. Both years use the identical equal-weight station set meeting 90% monthly coverage. Of 84 cells, 63 are supported, including 35 outside the four-month eight-year panel. The largest increase is October 2021 to 2022: +255.27 mm across 63 stations; the largest decrease is October 2022 to 2023: -301.58 mm across 59 stations. These extrema are descriptive, without significance or multiple-comparison testing. Changing local panels cannot be chained into a national trend or annual total; February also differs in length across leap years.

Selected continuous candidate: cdf_persistence_d8, development equal-threshold/year Brier 0.016812. Strong persistence baseline: 0.017939; climate baseline: 0.028989; global frequency: 0.029194; historical tree: 0.017585. Relative Brier reduction is 6.28% versus persistence and 42.41% versus frequency. These are probability-error reductions relative to named baselines, not accuracy improvements or superiority on every metric.

Training-only conditional empirical distributions provide P(S>h)=1-F(h) for finite nonnegative Decimal(38,18) thresholds. Right-sided CDF lookup makes equality negative. For tree candidates Spark DecisionTreeRegressor fits log1p future amount using past features, then exact training amounts form leaf CDFs. Fixed prior weights are 50 anchors for leaves and 200 for baseline groups. No validation outcomes are written back into predictions. This naturally gives threshold-specific probabilities and nonincreasing stepwise survival curves without post-hoc isotonic correction; arbitrary input is not a claim of a smooth curve. Nine checked cuts lie in 0..5 mm; arbitrary input acceptance and monotonicity do not prove every threshold or extreme-tail reliability. Beyond maximum training amount, empirical probability is zero with no proven tail guarantee.

All depth-8 CDF ablations hold rows, seed, min leaf size, bins, prior and shared feature ordering fixed. The threshold is a CDF query, not a tree input. Sequential differences test recent amount -> persistence -> periodic time -> station. Negative Brier delta means adding that group helps under this design; positive means it does not. These are model/data-conditional associations, not causal attribution. Impurity weights are separately saved but are not used as contribution evidence.

The original depth-5 threshold classifier, selected before its 2024 test, achieved mean Brier 0.01773592 against frequency 0.02888294, a 38.59% reduction on 6,338,426 eligible anchors per cut. Its development mean was 0.01758544. These are preserved historical classifier results and do not evaluate the current conditional-CDF model. The selected model used development folds only and refit through 2023; 2024 has not been rescored or used to select the new model. An independent later test would be needed to establish its final generalization performance.

Coverage-aware Task 1 supports seasonal occurrence/intensity differences, four fixed eight-year same-month comparisons and additional local adjacent-year contrasts. It does not establish national annual trends. Task 2 selects a continuous-threshold conditional distribution with development mean Brier 0.01681224, 6.28% lower than the stronger persistence baseline. Persistence features give a small model-conditional gain; calendar/station additions do not. Month and history-state diagnostics show heterogeneous error, and overlapping windows are correlated. Arbitrary input support does not establish every-threshold or live-deployment reliability.

OpenAI Codex was used throughout this project as an AI-assisted coding and documentation tool. Assistance included planning and implementing Spark data audits, cleaning and exact-time target construction; coverage-aware rainfall analysis and visualization; historical feature engineering; Spark MLlib model and conditional-distribution implementation; baseline, feature-ablation, temporal-validation, calibration and subgroup experiments; debugging, integrity and model-reload checks; and drafting, editing, translating and integrating the report, README and submission materials.

Inference assumes recorded historical rainfall is available at the event timestamp; actual publication latency is unverified. Missing rainfall is unknown, and comparisons/predictions apply to their documented coverage and input scope. Overlapping windows are not independent observations.
